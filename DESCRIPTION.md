# Working Villagers

**Hire villagers, pay them a wage and let them do the work.**

Working Villagers turns villagers into paid employees. Hand one a work contract, give it a workstation and a few chests, and it gets to work: felling trees, sawing planks, crafting chests, farming fields, smelting ore, digging pits, firing glass and bricks, selling goods to the villagers and carrying goods from one place to another. There are no machines. Every job is done by a villager walking around, opening chests and working at its station.

📖 **[Read the wiki](https://d4rkm0nkey.github.io/minecraft-working-villagers/)** for the full guide to every worker.

---

## 👷 The workers

| Worker | Workstation | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Lumberjack** | Chopping block | Fells natural trees around its block, replants them and brings the logs to a chest |
| **Sawyer** | Woodcutter | Cuts logs and planks into planks, slabs, stairs, fences, doors, signs, ... |
| **Stone sawyer** | Stonecutter | Cuts stone into stairs, slabs, walls, bricks, ... |
| **Carpenter** | Carpenter's bench | Crafts chests, barrels, ladders, tools, boats and other wooden goods |
| **Carrier** | Wicker basket | Carries items between chests along up to three routes |
| **Farmer** | Composter | Harvests and replants the fields around its composter |
| **Smith** | Blast furnace, smithing table | Smelts ore in the blast furnace, repairs the workers' worn tools at the smithing table |
| **Pit digger** | Pit stake | Digs out a pit you mark with string, layer by layer, with a ladder as the way out |
| **Kiln operator** | Kiln | Fires glass, stone, bricks, terracotta and charcoal in a brick kiln |
| **Shop assistant** | Trading counter | Sells goods from its chests to the villagers around and brings in emeralds |
| **Miner** | Mining bench | Digs a tunnel with supports into a hill, follows the ores and seals off water and lava |

The workers fit together. A lumberjack supplies the sawyer, and a carrier takes the planks to the carpenter, so you get a whole wood workshop that runs by itself.

## 📜 Hiring and wages

1. Craft a **work contract** (paper, ink sac and 3 emeralds) and right-click a villager with it. It's hired. A farmer, mason, shepherd, butcher, fisherman, armorer or toolsmith keeps its block, its trades and its level, and simply starts working for you. These workers still trade with you like before.
2. Place a **workstation**. The villager finds it by itself, walks there and takes the job (or you **assign** one in the menu).
3. **Link chests:** where the material comes from and where the products go.
4. **Right-click the worker with emeralds**, like giving a villager food. Every worker takes **one emerald a day** as its wage. No wage, no work. The lumberjack also needs an axe, the farmer a hoe and some seeds, the shepherd shears, the pit digger a shovel, a pickaxe and ladders.
5. During the day the villager works on its own.

Tired of handing out emeralds? Put wages, tools, saplings and seeds into a **village chest** (a chest plus an emerald). Every worker nearby takes what it runs low on by itself, before the first work of the day.

Hired villagers never take vanilla jobs and never quit by themselves. A worker that has no wage or no tool shakes its head at you, and its menu shows what it carries and what is missing. Outside work hours they still live like normal villagers: they sleep in beds, chat at the village bell and run from zombies and raids.

## 🪓 Lumberjack

- Looks for **natural trees** near its chopping block, chops them down, replants a sapling and takes the logs to a chest.
- Only touches trees with natural leaves, so **your log houses are safe**.
- Uses a real axe you give it. The axe wears out like yours, and better axes make it faster.
- Can **strip the logs** while felling if you switch it on.

## 🪚 Sawyer and stone sawyer

- The new **woodcutter** is a stonecutter for wood. One log gives 8 slabs or 4 stairs directly, without the crafting table's waste. You can use it yourself too.
- Give the sawyer an order (a product shape and the wood types it may use) and it cuts everything from its input chests.
- A hired villager at a vanilla **stonecutter** becomes a stone sawyer. Pick a product from any stone family and it does the rest. Ordinary villagers still become masons.

## 🔨 Carpenter

- The **carpenter's bench** is a crafting table for wooden things only: chests, barrels, ladders, bookshelves, wooden tools, boats, shields, bows, torches and more. Its menu lists everything and shows what you're missing.
- The carpenter crafts your order from its input chests and keeps a **stock** you choose, e.g. "always keep 16 chests ready".

## 🌾 Farmer, shepherd, butcher, fisher, fletcher, guard and smith

- A hired villager at a vanilla **composter** becomes a farmer: it harvests the ripe crops around it, replants them and brings the harvest to a chest. It needs a hoe you give it, never tills, never tramples the fields and touches nothing but crops.
- A hired villager at a vanilla **target** becomes a guard: at night it shoots the monsters around it with bow and arrows and picks up its arrows in the morning. Every shot costs an arrow, so the village needs a fletcher.
- A hired villager at a vanilla **fletching table** becomes a fletcher: it makes arrows, bows and crossbows and keeps a stock of them. It knaps the flint from gravel itself, or makes arrowheads from copper and iron nuggets.
- A hired villager at a vanilla **barrel** becomes a fisher: it fishes at the water nearby, and with a boat it rows out onto a lake or the sea, fishes there (treasure included, like a player on open water) and rows back with its catch.
- A hired villager at a vanilla **smoker** becomes a butcher: it keeps the cows, pigs, chickens and rabbits around it at the herd size you set, breeding below it and slaughtering above it (never named or leashed animals), and smokes the meat if you give it coal.
- A hired villager at a vanilla **loom** becomes a shepherd: it shears the sheep around it, even over the fence of a pen without a gate, and breeds them with wheat up to the herd size you set. The wool goes to a chest.
- A hired villager at a vanilla **blast furnace** becomes a smith: it loads the furnace with ore and fuel from its input chests and brings the ingots to its output chests.
- At a vanilla **smithing table** the smith repairs instead: the worn tools the workers give back into the village chests, and anything damaged in its input chests, with ingots, diamonds or planks like at an anvil, but without experience costs.
- Right-click the composter with an empty hand, or use the tabs above the loom or blast furnace screen, to set them up. Ordinary villagers still become vanilla farmers, shepherds and armorers there, but not at one you have set up.

## ⛏️ Pit digger and kiln operator

- Lay **string** on the ground in a closed ring and pick it at the **pit stake**: the pit digger digs out everything inside, layer by layer, down to the depth you set. It puts a ladder down the wall as it goes, never digs next to water or lava, leaves builds alone and brings every block it digs to its chest. Basements, foundations, sand and clay pits, or a simple quarry.
- The **kiln** is a brick furnace for building materials: glass, stone, smooth stone, bricks, terracotta, charcoal, ... It fires no food and no ore, so smoker, blast furnace and kiln each have their own job. The kiln operator fires everything from its input chests, or only what makes the product you pick, firing twice where needed (cobblestone to smooth stone), and keeps a stock you choose.

## ⛏️ Miner

- Pick a tunnel at the **mining bench** with one click on the side of a block: the miner digs a **3×3 tunnel** into the hill, with **wooden supports** every 4 blocks and torches, like a mineshaft. Side tunnels for branch mining or **stairs** down are a click away.
- It digs out the **ores** in the walls and the veins behind them, seals off **water and lava** with cobblestone, closes holes in the floor, and brings everything to its chest.
- For you, the mining bench is a **mill**: cobblestone to gravel, gravel to sand and flint. The stone from the mine becomes sand for glass.

## 💰 Shop assistant

- The **trading counter** turns a hired villager into a shop assistant. It sells what you put into its input chests to the villagers around, through their own trades: wheat to the farmer, sticks to the fletcher, paper to the librarian.
- Real vanilla trades: prices follow demand, trades sell out until the villagers restock, and the villagers level up from the trading.
- The emeralds go to its output chest. Finally a worker that earns the wages of the others.

## 🧺 Carrier

- Craft a **wicker basket** and hand it to a villager: it becomes a carrier, no contract needed.
- Set up to **three routes** from a source chest to a target chest. The carrier fills its basket, carries the items over and brings back what didn't fit.
- You can plan the routes on the basket before you hand it over.

## 🔍 Always know what's going on

- Every workstation's menu shows who works there. Give your workers names with a name tag.
- The menus show what each worker is doing right now: *Chopping a tree (40 %)*, *Cutting Oak Stairs*, *Waiting for wages*, *Done for today*, *Sleeping*, ...
- If a worker isn't working, the status tells you why.

## ⚙️ Data packs and configuration

- Woodcutter recipes and the carpenter's products are **data driven**, so data packs and other mods can add their own wood. The sawyer's orders only know the vanilla wood types for now.
- `config/workingvillagers.json` sets the wage, the distances, the speeds and the daily limits.

## Requirements

- Minecraft **26.3**
- **Fabric** (with [Fabric API](https://modrinth.com/mod/fabric-api)) or **NeoForge**

Needed on both client and server. Works in singleplayer and multiplayer.

## ⚠️ Beta

This is an early beta. **Back up your worlds** before trying it, and expect that saves may not carry over to later versions. Known limitations:

- All workers are novices for now. Levels are planned.
- Carriers have no filters yet and carry everything.
- Workers only work while their chunks are loaded.
- No JEI/EMI/REI or Jade integration yet.

Please report bugs and ideas on [GitHub](https://github.com/d4rkm0nkey/minecraft-working-villagers/issues).
